Irish citizenship by naturalisation is a long-term goal for many newcomers. The key question is **when** you become eligible. That depends on your **reckonable residence**: the time you have lived legally in Ireland on permissions that count. Here is how it works.

## What is reckonable residence?

Reckonable residence is time spent living in Ireland on an immigration permission that counts towards citizenship. Not every stamp counts. For example, time on Stamp 2 as a student is **not** reckonable, while time on Stamps 1, 1G, 3 and 4 is.

## The main rule: five years

Most adults applying based on residence need **five years** of reckonable residence. Counting back from the date you apply, you must show:

1. **365 days** of continuous residence immediately before you apply (366 if the period includes 29 February)
2. plus **1,460 days** in the eight years before that

That is 1,825 days in total over the last nine years (1,826 where a leap day is included).

## Travel during your final year

The final year must be continuous residence, but you are allowed some time abroad. You can be away for up to **70 days** in that year, with up to an extra 30 days allowed in exceptional circumstances, at the Minister's discretion.

## Other routes

- **Spouses and civil partners of Irish citizens** generally need three years of reckonable residence on the island of Ireland, plus other conditions.
- **Refugees and stateless people** may qualify after five years, counted in a different way.
- Applications on behalf of children and on the basis of Irish associations have their own rules.

## How to check your days

1. Gather every IRP card and permission letter you have had.
2. Note the start and end date of each permission and its stamp.
3. Use the official **Naturalisation Residency Calculator**, entering each period of permission.
4. Keep proof of residence for each year, such as tax, bank and utility documents with your name and address.

The calculator is a guide only. The official assessment happens when your application is lodged.

## FAQ

**Does time on a student visa count?**
No. Time on Stamp 2 isn't reckonable residence.

**Can I apply as soon as I reach five years?**
Only if the final year was continuous and within the travel allowance, and you meet the other conditions.

**Do EU citizens use the calculator?**
No. UK, EU, EEA and Swiss nationals prove their residence with documents instead.

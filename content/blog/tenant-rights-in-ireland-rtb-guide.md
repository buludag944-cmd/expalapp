Renting in Ireland is regulated by the Residential Tenancies Act, and the **Residential Tenancies Board (RTB)** oversees it. A tenancy agreement can add details, like who pays which bills, but it **can't take away your legal rights**. Here are the ones to know.

## Your tenancy must be registered

Your landlord must register your tenancy with the RTB **within one month** of it starting, and update it each year. You should get a confirmation letter from the RTB. You can check the register yourself, and if your tenancy isn't registered, you can report it.

## Deposits and advance rent

- There are legal limits on how much a landlord can ask for as a **security deposit** and as **rent in advance**. Check the RTB deposits page for the current limits.
- Get a written receipt for your deposit.
- At the end of the tenancy, the landlord must return the deposit **promptly**, minus only lawful deductions such as unpaid rent, bills or damage beyond normal wear and tear.

## Rent pressure zones

In a **Rent Pressure Zone (RPZ)**, rent increases are limited by law. If your home is in an RPZ, your landlord must tell you:

- the last rent amount set for the property
- the date it was last set
- how your rent was calculated using the RTB's RPZ calculator

The RPZ rules changed in March 2026, so use the RTB calculator and pages to check your situation.

## Proper notice before a tenancy ends

If your landlord wants to end your tenancy, they must follow the rules in the Act and give you a valid **Notice of Termination**. A text message or a verbal request isn't valid notice.

## Basic standards

Rented homes must meet minimum standards, for example for safety, heating and facilities. If something needs fixing, ask your landlord in writing and keep a copy.

## When something goes wrong

1. Raise the issue with your landlord in writing.
2. Keep copies of messages, receipts and photos.
3. If it isn't resolved, use the **RTB dispute resolution service**.

## FAQ

**Does my lease override my rights?**
No. A tenancy agreement can't remove your rights under rental law.

**Can I check if my tenancy is registered?**
Yes. Use the RTB's Check the Register service.

**Who do I contact about a deposit dispute?**
The RTB. Its dispute resolution service handles deposit and other tenancy disputes.

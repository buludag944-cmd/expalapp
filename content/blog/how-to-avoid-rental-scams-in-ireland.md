Finding a place to rent in Ireland is hard, and scammers know it. Newcomers are a common target because they often search from abroad and can't view a property in person. Here is how to spot a fake listing and protect your money.

## The most common warning signs

Be very careful if:

- the rent is much **lower** than similar places nearby
- the "landlord" says they are abroad and **can't show you the property**
- you are asked to pay a **deposit or first month's rent before viewing**
- they push you to pay quickly because "lots of people are interested"
- they ask for payment by money transfer services, crypto or gift cards
- the listing photos appear on other sites under a different address or name
- they will only talk by message and avoid phone or video calls

One sign alone doesn't prove a scam, but several together should stop you.

## Checks to make before you pay anything

1. **View the property**, in person or by a live video call where they walk through it. A pre-recorded video isn't enough.
2. **Search the photos** with a reverse image search to see if they appear elsewhere.
3. **Ask for ID and proof of ownership or agency**, and check the names match.
4. **Ask for a written tenancy agreement** before you pay.
5. **Pay by traceable bank transfer** to an account in the landlord's or agency's name, never cash or untraceable methods.
6. **Get a receipt** for any deposit or rent you pay.

## Know your deposit rights

In Ireland, your deposit is held by the landlord, who must return it promptly at the end of the tenancy, less any lawful deductions such as unpaid rent or damage beyond normal wear and tear. Landlords must also register tenancies with the Residential Tenancies Board (RTB) within one month of the tenancy starting.

## If you think you have been scammed

- Stop all contact and **don't send more money**.
- **Contact your bank** right away. They may be able to stop or recall a payment.
- **Report it to An Garda Síochána** at your local Garda station.
- Report the listing to the website where you found it.
- Keep screenshots of the ad, messages and payment receipts.

## FAQ

**Is it normal to pay a deposit in Ireland?**
Yes. A security deposit is normal, but only pay it once you have seen the property and have a written agreement.

**Can I check whether a tenancy is registered?**
Yes. The RTB lets you check whether a tenancy is on its register.

**Should I ever pay to view a property?**
No. Asking for money just to view a property is a red flag.

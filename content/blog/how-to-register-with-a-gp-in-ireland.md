A GP (general practitioner, or family doctor) is your first stop for most health problems in Ireland. Many GP practices have waiting lists, so it is worth finding one **before** you get sick. Here is how it works.

## How GPs work in Ireland

GPs in Ireland are mostly **private practitioners**. They run their own practices and decide whether to take on new patients. Unless you have a medical card or a GP visit card, you pay for each visit. Prices vary between practices, so ask when you call.

Some groups can get free GP visits. For example, people with a medical card or GP visit card, and children under a certain age. The HSE website explains who qualifies.

## Step 1: Find practices near you

Use the HSE **Find a GP** service. Search by town, county or Eircode to see practices with their addresses and phone numbers.

Ask people nearby too. Colleagues, neighbours and other newcomers often know which practices are taking new patients.

## Step 2: Call and ask the right questions

When you call a practice, ask:

1. are you taking new patients?
2. what does a standard visit cost?
3. do I need to fill in a registration form, and what ID should I bring?
4. how do I book appointments, and is there an out-of-hours service?

## Step 3: Register and send your records

Fill in the practice's registration form. If you have medical records or a list of medicines from your home country, bring a copy. This helps your new GP understand your history.

## If practices are full

If you can't find a GP who will take you:

- widen your search to nearby areas
- ask to join a waiting list at more than one practice
- if you have a medical card or GP visit card and have been refused by **three** GPs, you can apply to the HSE to be assigned one

## Out-of-hours and emergencies

GP out-of-hours services cover evenings, nights and weekends in most areas. For a medical emergency, call **112 or 999**.

## FAQ

**Is GP care free in Ireland?**
Not for everyone. Most adults pay per visit unless they have a medical card or GP visit card.

**Do I need a PPS number to register with a GP?**
Practices may ask for one, especially for medical card patients. Ask the practice when you call.

**Can I see a GP before I register?**
Some practices see non-registered patients, but many prioritise their own patients. Ask when you book.

Irish winters are usually mild compared with many countries, but they are dark, wet and windy, and storms can close roads, cancel transport and cut power. A little preparation makes your first winter much easier.

## Understand weather warnings

Met Éireann, the national weather service, issues colour-coded warnings when severe weather is expected, usually within a 48-hour window:

- **Status Yellow: be aware.** Weather that is dangerous in some places or for some activities.
- **Status Orange: be prepared.** Weather that could significantly affect people in the area.
- **Status Red: take action.** Protect yourself and your property and follow official advice. This may mean staying indoors.

## Stay informed

1. Install the **Met Éireann app** and turn on warning notifications.
2. Check the Met Éireann warnings page before travelling.
3. Follow your local council and transport operators, as they post closures and cancellations.
4. Visit the government's Be Winter Ready site for advice.

## Prepare your home

- Keep a torch, spare batteries and a phone power bank in case of power cuts.
- Know where your electricity meter and water stopcock are.
- Bring in or tie down bins, garden furniture and bikes before a storm.
- Tell your landlord early about leaks, damp or heating problems, and do it in writing.
- Save the phone numbers of neighbours, your landlord and family.

## If you drive

- Get your car serviced before winter.
- Check your tyres. The legal minimum tread for a car is **1.6mm**, but at least **3mm** is advised for winter.
- Check road conditions before you set out, and think about postponing your journey during severe weather.

## Look after yourself

Short winter days can feel long when you're new and far from home. Get outside in daylight when you can, keep a routine, and stay in touch with people. Meeting other newcomers helps.

## Emergencies

For an emergency, call **112 or 999**. Both numbers work from any phone.

## FAQ

**Does it snow much in Ireland?**
Not often, especially near the coast, but cold snaps with ice and some snow happen. Wind and rain are more common problems.

**What should I do during a red warning?**
Follow official advice, which usually means avoiding unnecessary travel and staying indoors.

**Where can I see current warnings?**
On the Met Éireann website and app.

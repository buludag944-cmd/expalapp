Many newcomers get a shock with their first Irish payslip: a big chunk has gone on **emergency tax**. This usually happens because Revenue doesn't know about the job yet. The fix is quick, and you can do it yourself online.

## Why emergency tax happens

Your employer needs a **Revenue Payroll Notification (RPN)** to tax you correctly. The RPN tells them your tax credits and rate band. If there is no RPN for you, your employer must use emergency tax. Emergency tax is higher than normal tax because you get no credits.

For your **first job** in Ireland, you must register the job with Revenue yourself. For later jobs, your employer usually registers the job for you.

## What you need

- your **PPS number**
- a **Revenue myAccount** login
- your employer's name and **employer registration number**, which HR or payroll can give you
- your start date

## Step 1: Get your PPS number

You can't register a job without a PPS number. If you don't have one yet, apply as soon as you arrive. See our guide on [getting a PPS number](https://expalapp.netlify.app/blog/how-to-get-a-pps-number-in-ireland).

## Step 2: Register for myAccount

Register on the Revenue website with your PPS number and contact details. Revenue sends some security details by post, so allow a few days.

## Step 3: Add your job

1. Log in to myAccount.
2. Go to **PAYE Services**.
3. Choose to add job or pension details.
4. Enter your employer's details and your start date, then submit.

Revenue then issues an RPN to your employer.

## Step 4: Check your payslip

Your next payslip should show normal tax. If you paid emergency tax earlier in the year, it is usually refunded through payroll once your credits apply. If it isn't, contact Revenue through myAccount.

## Common mistakes

- Starting work before you have a PPS number, then forgetting to register the job.
- Assuming your employer has registered your first job. For a first job, you must do it.
- Ignoring the post from Revenue with your myAccount details.

## FAQ

**Do I get emergency tax back?**
Usually, yes. Once Revenue has your job details, overpaid tax is normally refunded through your pay.

**Do I need to register every new job?**
Only your first job. After that, your employer registers new jobs with Revenue.

**Can I do this before my first day?**
You can register the job once you have the employer's details and your start date.
